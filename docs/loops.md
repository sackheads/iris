# Loops

`/loop 10m check my open PRs` re-runs a prompt in the same conversation on a cadence, with ticks you can watch, interrupt and talk back to.  Leave the interval out (`/loop check my open PRs`) and it's self-paced: Iris picks each next wait, from a minute to an hour, and 20 minutes when it doesn't say.  Talking to a looping conversation, or a peer's message to it, runs a turn at once and never moves the next tick: an interval loop ticks every interval from its last tick's start, and a self-paced one when it last asked to, so a conversation that hears from peers more often than its interval still runs its own prompt.  A tick that falls due while a turn runs goes once, right after that turn, with no catch-up for the ones it missed (#1423).  A chip above the composer shows the cadence, the ticks left and when the next is due, with Pause, Resume and Stop.  Ticks run attended (an approval waits for you, as under `/goal`), each one resends the conversation so far (compacted once it grows past a budget, below; on Anthropic, cached for an hour when the loop ticks every 45 minutes or less, #1437), and a loop stops at the Loop tick limit (Settings → Advanced → Agent Limits, default 50).  Once a loop's or goal's history passes the compaction budget (same section, default 48k tokens, at most half the model's context), its next turn replaces the older turns with one summary, keeping the recent ones, every message you typed, and your decision on every Inbox answer you gave (its option and your words, never the item's own text), word for word (#1425). A timed veto's default that applied with nobody answering is summarised, not quoted as yours.  The transcript you see keeps everything, and a line says when it happened.  On a conversation that has read outside text, the summary is treated as outside text too: guard-checked and wrapped.  If no summary can be made, nothing is compacted until the history nears the model's limit, when the oldest turns are dropped with a notice and a count on the session's card (#1421).  It comes back paused after a relaunch.  Iris can also set a one-shot **alarm** (`set_alarm`, 15 seconds to an hour) instead of a blocking `sleep` when it has to wait for a build or a CI run: it ends its turn, the session stays free, and the alarm starts a new turn in the same conversation with Iris's note.  An alarm uses the conversation's one loop slot, so it is refused while your own `/loop` runs, shows on the same chip (Cancel alarm), and, like a loop, comes back paused after a relaunch (Resume then counts its whole delay again).  Another turn in the conversation does not move it.  For work that should run with nobody watching, use a [job](jobs.md).  Details: [slash commands](slash_commands.md), [spec](specs/2026-10-19-loop.md). Every gated call in a looping conversation, and what each tick spent in tokens, is logged to `logs/loop-approvals.jsonl` (shapes and counts, never arguments; rotated at 5 MB) to measure what unattended loop mode (#1425) has to handle.
